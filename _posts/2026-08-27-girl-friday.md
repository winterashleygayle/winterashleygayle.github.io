# Girl Friday
<div class="alchemy-divider"></div>

Lately, I’ve had a really interesting couple of weeks and several unexpected, really positive run-ins with elderly gentlemen who could easily be father or grandfather figures to me.

It’s made me realize something about myself that I hadn’t fully understood before: I have mommy wounds, but my daddy wound feels less like a wound and more like a hole, which is a wound. There just… wasn’t really a father there.

As I’ve gotten older, I’ve become increasingly aware of all the things in myself that I want to heal. But for a long time, I honestly felt overwhelmed by it. My plate was already so full, and I knew I had these things to work through, but I didn’t always know what to work on first, or even how to begin.

And lately I’ve been thinking a lot about something I’ve heard for years from Wayne Dyer and Abraham Hicks.

My mom introduced me to Abraham Hicks about 15 years ago. At the time, I needed something, specifically, I needed a feminine voice that felt soothing and nurturing rather than harsh. I loved Esther Hicks’ voice, and I kept listening. Over time, Abraham became a sort of spiritual grandmother to me.

I discovered Wayne Dyer through her, and I immediately connected with his teachings. I’ve always affectionately thought of him as my spiritual grandpa.

So, in my head, Abraham Hicks and Wayne Dyer became my grandma and grandpa in spirit.

Wayne Dyer passed away in 2015, but I still have one of his CDs that I inherited from my mom when she passed. It’s called The Power of Intention.

It took me a long time to really understand what intention meant to me.

I’ve realized that I’ve had a very deep, almost silent intention my entire life: to survive and thrive. To overcome. To keep going. To build something out of whatever I was handed.

And maybe that intention has been there longer than I realized.

So this brings me to the last couple of weeks.

At the tax office where I work, we have a handful of very successful local businessmen who have been clients for years. They’re investors, business owners, and people I used to find incredibly intimidating.

In my younger mind, they represented this very stereotypical image of the capitalist patriarchy, the kind of powerful older men who might look at me as “just the secretary” and expect me to fetch coffee.

The funny thing is…none of them have ever actually treated me that way.

Over the years, I’ve gotten to know some of them a little personally. They remember my name. They joke with me. They call me “the boss.” They’ve always been respectful and surprisingly sweet.

But I never really thought much about what that meant to me until yesterday.

Twice in one day, I had encounters with two of these men.

The first called the office looking for my boss, who is currently on vacation. I told him when John would be back, and there was a little pause.

Then he said, “You know, I’m really proud of you, Ashley. You’re putting yourself through school and doing something with yourself.”

That stopped me.

It made me feel seen.

Not as an employee. Not as a secretary. Not as someone who was simply doing her job.

But as a daughter might feel seen by a father.

Then he told me he was a feminist.

He said, “Do you know what makes me a feminist? It’s a pop quiz, but it’s only one word.”

I jokingly said, “Your wife?”

He said, “Daughters.”

😂

He told me his daughters had made him a feminist, that he was proud of me, and that I reminded him of them. He told me to keep going.

And it honestly meant more to me than he probably realized.

About an hour later, another longtime client came into the office. He sat down on the steps with me, and we ended up talking for about 20 minutes.

He was dealing with some very personal things in his own life and was vulnerable with me about what he was going through. Then he asked what I was doing these days.

I told him I’m finishing my accounting degree and plan to pursue my CPA license.

His response was basically, “You need to do this. Yes. Absolutely.”

He told me I had built something there. He called me an asset to the team. I told him some of my long-term plans and what I hope to do when my boss eventually retires, and he was incredibly supportive.

And I walked away from both conversations thinking:

What are the odds?

Two older men, in the same day, both of whom I once found intimidating, both showing me kindness, encouragement, respect, and genuine belief in what I’m doing.

And both, in completely different ways, stepping into a role that felt very fatherly.

It made me think about intention in a different way.

Maybe healing doesn’t always look like sitting down and consciously figuring out how to repair every broken part of ourselves.

Maybe sometimes it looks like saying:

“God. Source. Spirit. Whatever you are — I know there’s something in me that needs healing, but I honestly don’t know how to heal it. I don’t even know where to start. Please help me.”

And then letting go.

I’ve always loved the idea behind Ask, and it is Given.

Not necessarily as some scientifically proven formula because I know these are spiritual and esoteric ideas, not scientific facts — but as an expression of faith.

Faith that we don’t have to understand the entire path before taking the next step.

Faith that sometimes help arrives in ways we couldn’t have planned.

Faith that maybe the intention itself matters.

And maybe, just maybe, we are being taken care of in ways we don’t always recognize while they’re happening.

I don’t know if these two encounters were “signs.” I don’t know if my angels arranged anything. I don’t know if there is some cosmic explanation for it.

But I know what it felt like.

It felt like something in me that had been empty for a very long time got quietly acknowledged.

And I’m grateful.

Maybe that’s what healing looks like sometimes.

Not forcing it.

Not figuring it all out.

Just asking for help…setting the intention…and paying attention when life answers in unexpected ways. ❤️

# about that girl friday...

For years, I might have heard that term and thought it meant the woman behind the scenes who does everything for everyone else.

But maybe there’s another way to look at it.

I’m a Girl Friday.
I’m a Jane of all trades.
I can work a tax return. I can put together a spreadsheet. I can work with computers and AI. I can create art. I can figure out technology. I can go back to school and work toward an accounting degree and a CPA license.
I can change the water pump in my Jeep.
I can turn a wrench.
I can take care of myself.
I can take care of people.
I can survive tax season.
I can learn something completely new and figure it out.

I’ve done a lot of things in my life.
And when I look at the whole picture, I realize something:
I’ve already lived a very full life.
Not a perfect one. Not an easy one.
But a full one.



I’ve adapted. I’ve learned. I’ve worked. I’ve cared for people. I’ve built things. I’ve started over. I’ve gone back to school. I’ve reinvented myself more than once.

And I’m damn proud of that.

Maybe the intention I’ve carried all these years wasn’t simply to survive.
Maybe it was to become someone who could walk into almost any situation and say:

“Okay. I don’t know how to do this yet…but I’ll figure it out.”
